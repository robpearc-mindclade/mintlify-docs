This architecture decision record drafts biosecurity and dual-use controls for Mindclade's programmable biology roadmap. It is for scientists, security reviewers, legal counsel, and anyone assessing the safety boundaries around generative design and automated experimentation.

## Context

The north star includes generative design for programmable biology and, later, automated experiment execution. The [2026-09-26 design audit](../reviews/2026-09-26-design-audit.md) found no dual-use or biosecurity policy anywhere in the architecture package, while [programmable biology](../programmable-biology.md) already plans Clade model families, experiment protocols, and laboratory adapters. That is the only safety gap the audits identified, and Rob asked for this draft first among the science-gate ADRs.

The gap matters because screening effectiveness is not static. Work published in 2025 evaluated open-source AI protein-design software against the biosecurity screening tools used by nucleic-acid synthesis providers, found that AI-redesigned sequences were not reliably detected, and then developed and deployed patches that improved detection (Wittmann et al., _Science_ 390(6768):82 through 87, 2025-10-02, [doi:10.1126/science.adu8578](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adu8578); retrieved through PubMed). The lesson this ADR takes from it is procedural: a screening step is only as good as the tool version behind it, so the version and its result belong in the evidence record.

This ADR is policy, at the same level as the existing laws it builds on: [ADR-005](adr-005-evidence-claim-policy-and-decision-law.md) for evidence, claims, policy, and decisions, [ADR-009](adr-009-independent-assurance.md) for assurance that the implementer does not own, [ADR-011](adr-011-agent-capability-attenuation.md) for attenuated capabilities, and [ADR-006](adr-006-biological-world-model-authority.md) for canonical biological meaning.

## Decision

Hazard screening is a precondition for any generative-design capability and for any laboratory or experiment-execution adapter. Five controls follow; each needs its own reviewed criteria and evidence contract before the capability it guards exists.

### 1. Screening before synthesis or ordering

A designed sequence must pass hazard screening before it can be synthesized, ordered, or sent to an external provider. A missing, failed, or inconclusive screening result blocks the request; it is not a warning the requester can proceed past. The screening tool and its version are recorded with the decision, because detection quality changes with tool revisions, and a screening result is evidence bound to that version rather than a permanent property of the sequence. Re-screening is required when the tool version, the design model, or the sequence changes.

### 2. Access control on models and outputs

Generative-design models, their weights and checkpoints, and the designs they produce are capability-scoped under ADR-011: explicit grants, no ambient authority, no privilege inherited by a child agent. Designs are artifacts with a restricted audience, and a handoff, report, or projection may not widen that audience. Public availability of an external model does not establish permitted use.

### 3. Human authorization for any wet-lab action

Every physical-world action (synthesis, ordering, or execution through a laboratory adapter) requires a named human authorization bound to the exact request, the screening decision, and the policy revision in force. There is no agent-only path, and no accumulated approval: one authorization covers one request. Authorization can be withheld, paused, or withdrawn, and a run may not edit the policy or the screening configuration that governs it.

### 4. Logging and audit

Screening requests and outcomes, tool and model versions, authorizer identity, the target provider or facility, and refusals are all recorded as retained evidence. Refusals are kept, not discarded, because a pattern of refused requests is itself a signal. The audit record is reviewable by an assurance owner who is not the implementer, per ADR-009.

### 5. Alignment with evolving norms, cited only where verified

Mindclade tracks external frameworks as inputs; the internal precondition above does not depend on which version is in force.

- **United States:** the 2023 HHS _Screening Framework Guidance for Providers and Users of Synthetic Nucleic Acids_, which sets out recommended baseline standards for providers ([ASPR](https://www.aspr.gov/S3/Pages/Synthetic-Nucleic-Acids.aspx)).
- **United States:** the OSTP _Framework for Nucleic Acid Synthesis Screening_ of 2024-04-29, under which recipients of federal life-sciences research funding procure synthetic nucleic acids and benchtop synthesizers only from compliant providers ([ASPR](https://www.aspr.gov/S3/Pages/OSTP-Framework-for-Nucleic-Acid-Synthesis-Screening.aspx)).
- **United States:** Executive Order 14292, _Improving the Safety and Security of Biological Research_, signed 2025-05-05, whose section 4(b) directs OSTP to revise or replace that 2024 framework within 90 days, with enforcement mechanisms and review at least every four years ([Federal Register document 2025-08266](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/html/2025/05/08/2025-08266.html)). As of 2026-09-26 this author did not verify a published replacement, so the successor framework's content is an open external dependency.
- **United Kingdom:** _UK screening guidance on synthetic nucleic acids_, published by the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology on 2024-10-08 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-screening-guidance-on-synthetic-nucleic-acids)).
- **Community tooling:** IBBIS publishes the Common Mechanism, a free, open-source screening tool that providers run locally ([IBBIS](https://ibbis.bio/our-work/common-mechanism/)).

Any adopted screening mechanism, provider, or tool is a separate decision with its own review; naming these sources here selects none of them.

### Non-goals

This ADR is not a biosafety manual, a containment or facility procedure, or an incident-response plan. It contains no hazard information: no agents, sequences, thresholds, hazard lists, or anything about how screening could be evaded, and the repository does not become a place where such material is stored. It does not approve any laboratory adapter, synthesis provider, or screening vendor, does not authorize generative design or experiment execution, and is not a legal compliance opinion or a claim of compliance with any framework. It changes no contract, Codegen, or graph law.

## Consequences

- Any future gate that introduces a generative-design capability or a laboratory or experiment-execution adapter must name hazard screening among its acceptance criteria and its stop conditions. A gate that omits it is rejected at criteria review.
- Nothing currently implemented is blocked: no design capability and no laboratory adapter exists today, so this ADR is a precondition on future work rather than a change to present behaviour.
- A subsequent ADR must select the concrete screening mechanism and define the screening evidence and decision records before the S-gates in the [roadmap](../implementation-roadmap.md#gate-sequence) can close. That ADR owns the operational detail this one deliberately omits.
- Screening decisions become part of the evidence, claim, policy, and decision model rather than a side channel: a screening pass is evidence, and eligibility to order remains a separate recorded decision.
- Being external, the referenced frameworks can change without notice. A change to them is an input to review, never an automatic change to Mindclade policy.
- Status stays **Proposed** until Rob accepts it. Until then no gate may cite it as an accepted law, and no capability may claim it as satisfied.
